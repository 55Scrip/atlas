## 1. Product Thesis

Atlas is a private investment system for people who care about capital.

It is built for serious private capital owners, entrepreneurs, business owners,
and long-term investors who have money, ideas, responsibility, and questions —
but who want to make investment decisions with more structure, less noise, and
less impulse.

Atlas is not another stock app.

Atlas is not a trading product.

Atlas is not an AI stock-picker.

Atlas turns messy investment input into structured judgment.

## 3. Primary User

Atlas's first user is the serious private capital owner.

This person may be:

- an entrepreneur
- a business owner
- a high-income professional
- a long-term private investor
- someone who has built or received capital and wants to protect it
- someone with several holdings, ideas, watchlist names, notes, and open
  decisions

They often have capital to allocate, but no clear investment process.

They may read a lot, follow markets, gather ideas, and care deeply about
outcomes — but their thinking is fragmented across notes, broker screens,
screenshots, articles, messages, watchlists, and memory.

They are too engaged to simply ignore investing.

But they do not want to live inside dashboards, trading apps, alerts, charts,
and market noise.

They want a system.

---

## 4. Secondary User

Atlas can also help people who are held back from investing by uncertainty,
fear, or lack of knowledge.

Many people never take the first step because investing feels confusing, risky,
or reserved for people who already know what they are doing.

Private banking is available only to people who already have significant
capital.

Most beginners are left to learn the hard way, or are told to simply buy a
fund without understanding the process.

Atlas should make the ramp into investing easier.

Not by telling people what to buy.

Not by hiding risk.

Not by pretending investing is simple.

But by showing how a thoughtful investment decision can be structured:

- What do I own?
- Why might it fit?
- What evidence is missing?
- What risks should I monitor?
- What questions should I answer before acting?
- When is waiting reasonable?

---

## 5. Core Problem

The core problem is not lack of information.

The core problem is lack of structured judgment.

Investors have more information than ever:

- broker screens
- news
- social media
- podcasts
- analyst notes
- screenshots
- watchlists
- charts
- opinions
- fear
- FOMO
- partial knowledge

But they often lack a repeatable process for turning that input into better
decisions.

This leads to:

- impulsive decisions
- unclear reasoning
- weak follow-up
- scattered research
- overconfidence
- fear-based inaction
- buying too quickly
- selling too quickly
- forgetting why something was considered
- failing to distinguish evidence from noise
- feeling that they "should have better control"

Atlas exists to solve this.

---

## 6. Product Promise

Atlas helps users understand their decision state before they act.

It does not promise better predictions.

It does not promise market timing.

It does not promise winners.

It promises structure.

The core promise:

Atlas helps you see what is clear, what is missing, what needs more evidence,
what should be revisited, and when no action is warranted.

---

## 7. What Atlas Does

Atlas helps users:

- structure portfolios
- structure watchlists
- organize research notes
- identify missing evidence
- surface open decisions
- create decision journals
- review risks
- identify reasons to wait
- run weekly investment reviews
- reduce impulsive decisions
- learn how better investment decisions are built
- feel calmer, clearer, and more in control

Atlas turns investment input into a workspace.

---

## 8. What Atlas Does Not Do

Atlas does not give buy/sell signals.

Atlas does not execute trades.

Atlas does not set price targets.

Atlas does not try to time the market.

Atlas does not replace professional financial advice.

Atlas does not claim certainty.

Atlas does not promise outperformance.

Atlas is not a trading app.

Atlas is not a stock-picking machine.

Atlas is not a broker.

Atlas is not another dashboard full of charts.

Atlas is not private banking.

Atlas is not a shortcut around judgment.

Atlas builds the process.

## 9. First User Experience

Atlas should be input-first, not dashboard-first.

The first screen should not begin with:

- create account
- connect broker
- choose plan
- configure dashboard
- import everything
- answer a long questionnaire

The first screen should begin with input.

### First-screen prompt

Paste a portfolio, watchlist, research note, or open investment decision.

Atlas will structure it into a temporary workspace.

### Swedish

Klistra in en portfölj, bevakningslista, analysanteckning eller ett öppet
investeringsbeslut.

Atlas strukturerar det till en tillfällig arbetsyta.

### First flow

Paste → Temporary Workspace → Structured Review → Save Optional

Account creation should appear only after the user has seen value.

---

## 10. No-Account First Value

Atlas should be useful before asking for trust.

A user should be able to paste investment-related input and receive a structured
temporary workspace before creating an account.

The account prompt should be framed around persistence:

- save this workspace
- continue later
- keep history
- build a decision journal
- access across devices
- collaborate later

Account creation should not block first value.

Broker connection should not be required for first value.

## 13. Pricing Philosophy

Atlas should optimize for genuine long-term users first, not maximum early
revenue per user.

The product should be priced low enough to build a large base of serious weekly
users, but high enough to signal trust, quality, and seriousness.

Atlas should not be a cheap stock-tip app.

Atlas should not be an inaccessible private-banking product.

It should sit in the middle: a serious investment system for people who care
about capital.

### Pricing v1 hypothesis

| Tier | Price |
|---|---|
| Guest | 0 kr/month |
| Personal | 99–199 kr/month |
| Plus | 299–499 kr/month |

### Guest

For first value:

- paste input
- receive temporary workspace
- limited structured review
- no saved history
- no long-term journal

### Personal

For serious private investors:

- saved workspaces
- decision journal
- watchlist structure
- weekly review
- history
- basic export
- more input types

### Plus

For more active private capital owners:

- multiple portfolios
- deeper review history
- more saved workspaces
- more advanced journal
- richer follow-up
- future priority features

Atlas should not exceed the private-user ceiling too early.

The goal is volume of genuine subscribers, trust, and habit — not extracting
the maximum from a few early users.

## 18. Strategic Standard

Atlas must be the best product of its kind, or it should not be built.

That does not mean the first version must have every feature.

It means every feature must serve the core promise:

- clearer thinking
- better structure
- less impulse
- better follow-up
- more control
- more understandable investing

If a feature adds noise, it does not belong.

If a feature encourages impulsive action, it does not belong.

If a feature makes Atlas feel like another stock app, it does not belong.

If a feature helps users think better before acting, it belongs.
