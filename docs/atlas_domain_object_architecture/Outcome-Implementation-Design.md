# Outcome — Implementation Design

This document is an implementation-design artifact, not a normative document. It carries no Doctrine status (Draft/Final/Superseded/Historical) because those statuses apply to normative documents, and this is not one. Where anything here appears to conflict with the Architecture Doctrine, OE-002 through OE-006, or the Historical Decision Record, those documents govern and this one is wrong and must be corrected.

## 1. Executive Finding

**Outcome is a Final-adopted Domain Object (OE-002 §5.6), and is implementation-ready.** Its exact one-sentence meaning: **a permanent, independently-identified, Case-owned Domain Object that records a determinate state of affairs which the Case treats as having become actual — without asserting objective truth, causal attribution, success, failure, or measurement.** The content or relation that constitutes it: a single, unified "realized matter" slot, realized as internal free-text content, a typed reference to another already-accepted same-Case Domain Object, or (unresolved, and therefore currently unadmitted) both at once — the identical structural pattern OE-002 §5.6 itself states Outcome follows "the same pattern as Judgment and Decision." **Selected outcome: Outcome 5 — Ready With Alternative Content Forms**, using the corrected Storage-Permissive, Admission-Restricted contract already established for Decision.

**Exact minimal field set**: `id`, `case_id`, `realized_content` (free text, nullable) and/or `matter_target_type`/`matter_target_id` (typed reference, nullable pair) with at least one realization required and simultaneous presence structurally representable but not currently domain-admitted, an optional `occurred_at` (the time the realized matter is treated as having become actual, distinct from and never conflated with `recorded_at`), and `recorded_at` (acceptance/event time). No Agent, Decision-reference requirement, success/failure field, or measurement field is included — each is directly and explicitly excluded by OE-002 §5.6's own text, more decisively than for any other type investigated in this series.

## 2. Scope

This document designs Outcome's implementation within `docs/atlas_domain_object_architecture/`. It does not redesign Case, identity, or the validation/acceptance model. It does not alter the completed Knowledge Reference, Reasoning Trace, Reasoning Act, Judgment, or Decision designs. It reuses the corrected Decision Implementation Design's own committed-to-matter analysis directly, since OE-002 §5.6 itself states Outcome's realized matter follows the identical structural pattern — this document does not re-derive that discipline from first principles a third time, but does independently re-verify it applies to Outcome's own text before relying on it (Section 32).

## 4. Repository Existence and Adoption Check

Outcome is unambiguously **Final-adopted**: the sixth and last of the six named types in OE-002 §4's closed Domain Object Set, with its own dedicated definition in §5.6, its own event (`OutcomeAccepted`, OE-003 §4.6), and no open question about its *existence* — only about one specific internal edge (the internal-versus-referential coexistence question), exactly parallel in kind to Judgment's and Decision's own analogous open questions.

## 5. Explicit Canonical Claims

OE-002 §5.6, quoted in full:

> **Definition.** Outcome is a permanent Domain Object that records a determinate state of affairs which the Case treats as having become actual, without asserting objective truth or attributing that realization to a specific cause.
>
> **Identity.** Outcome has stable identity independent of its content and independent of the identity of any object it references.
>
> **Responsibility.** Outcome is responsible for recording that a state of affairs is treated as realized, as distinct from intended, possible, or proposed matter. Outcome does not assert causality, success, failure, or measurement.
>
> **Ownership boundary.** Outcome's realized matter MAY be content held internally by the Outcome itself, or a reference to another Domain Object belonging to the same Case. Both forms are independently valid. This document does not establish that both forms may coexist within one undifferentiated Outcome; this is an open question under Section 7 of the Architecture Doctrine. Outcome's root-eligibility depends on which form a given instance uses, following the same pattern as Judgment and Decision.
>
> **Relationships.** Where Outcome's realized matter is a reference, it MUST be to another same-Case Domain Object. No specific Domain Object type is required. Outcome does not require a Decision, a Judgment, or a Reasoning Trace. Outcome MAY be referenced by a Decision or by a Reasoning Trace as one permissible target among others; this document does not establish Outcome as the required or exclusively endorsed target of either. Outcome may be recorded retrospectively: the time at which the realized matter is treated as having become actual need not equal the time of Outcome's own acceptance. A later Outcome does not erase or invalidate an earlier one.

Additional directly relevant claims: OE-002 §3 — "No Domain Object defined in this document requires an Agent, requires multiple alternatives, asserts objective or externally verified truth, or asserts causal attribution, except where explicitly stated otherwise below" (no exception is stated for Outcome anywhere in §5.6, so this blanket rule applies to Outcome unmodified). OE-003 §4.6 (`OutcomeAccepted`) — "Fact established: that a specific Outcome has been accepted, recording that a state of affairs is treated by the Case as having become actual. Not established: the objective truth of the realized matter; any cause of the realization; that the event's own acceptance time coincides with the time the realized matter is treated as having occurred." OE-003 §7 — "An Outcome's event MAY occur at any time relative to the time its own content treats the realized matter as having become actual; retrospective recording does not violate any ordering rule stated here." OE-003 §9 — "Where a Domain Object's content treats some matter as having occurred at an earlier point — as Outcome's own definition in OE-002 permits — the event representing that object's acceptance MUST still be understood as timed at acceptance, not at the represented time." OE-004 INV-015 — "Does not require... represented time... coincide with, precede, or follow acceptance time in any particular way beyond what OE-002 and OE-003 already permit for Outcome." OE-004 INV-012 — Outcome's root eligibility is conditional on its chosen form, exactly mirroring Judgment's and Decision's own text.

## 6. Required Distinctions

- **Outcome** — as defined above: a realized state of affairs, not a characterization (contrast Judgment), not a practical commitment (contrast Decision), not a reliance-on-knowledge (contrast Knowledge Reference), not a support relation (contrast Reasoning Trace), not preserved informational content agnostic to truth (contrast Observation, which preserves content "without asserting that the content is true" — a different disclaimer serving a different purpose than Outcome's own truth/causation/success/measurement exclusions).
- **Result versus process** — Outcome records that a state of affairs *is treated as realized*; it does not record a stepwise process, a chronology, or an occurrence-event in the generic Domain-Event sense (Section 11). This is a genuine, textually supplied exception to the architecture's general process-exclusion boundary, not an invented one.
- **Success/failure/measurement/causation** — each is directly, explicitly excluded by Outcome's own Responsibility clause, more decisively than any exclusion required for any other type in this series (Sections 21, 22, 24).
- **Observation** — preserves informational content without asserting truth; Outcome records realized matter without asserting truth, causation, success, or measurement. These are independently adopted, textually distinct types (Section 17); Outcome is not "the observational" type, and treating it as such would import unsupported epistemic/perceptual semantics not stated by OE-002 (Section 17).
- **Learning, Evaluation** — non-governing, pre-OE-002 legacy CoreLoop concepts (`atlas/core/domain/learning/entity.py`, `atlas/core/domain/evaluation/entity.py`); not part of OE-002 §4's closed set; not imported into Outcome's ontology (Section 20).

## 7. Candidate Designs

**Candidate A — Free-Text Result.** Strongest argument: matches OE-002's own permitted internal-content form directly. Canonical evidence: "realized matter MAY be content held internally by the Outcome itself." **Disposition: Adopted, as one of two alternative realizations of the single realized-matter slot** (Section 32) — not as the sole or mandatory form.

**Candidate B — Decision Result Record.** Tested precisely: is a Decision relation *mandatory*, or does Outcome record execution of a Decision? No — OE-002 states directly "Outcome does not require a Decision, a Judgment, or a Reasoning Trace," and separately states Outcome's Definition "without... attributing that realization to a specific cause" — foreclosing any causal-execution reading. **Disposition: Rejected as mandatory, as an execution record, or as a causal claim**; a Decision reference remains adopted only as one generic, non-exclusive, non-required target example (Section 14).

**Candidate C — Target Plus Result Characterization.** Tested directly against the grammar-comparison method already established for Decision versus Judgment (Section 32): OE-002 §5.6 names a single concept, "realized matter," not two separately required components (a mandatory target *plus* a separately mandatory content field). **Disposition: Rejected** as textually unsupported — Outcome's own grammar mirrors Decision's one-part structure, not Judgment's two-part structure.

**Candidate D — Alternative Content Forms.** Directly and precisely adopted: the two forms are alternatives realizing one slot, exactly as OE-002's own Ownership Boundary clause states, explicitly "following the same pattern as Judgment and Decision," with their possible coexistence explicitly and deliberately left open. **Disposition: Adopted as the selected structure** (Section 9, Section 32), inheriting the corrected Decision Implementation Design's own minimum-presence/storage-permissive/domain-restrictive contract.

**Candidate E — Measurement Record.** Contradiction: directly and explicitly excluded — "Outcome does not assert... measurement." **Disposition: Rejected outright** (Section 22).

**Candidate F — Success/Failure Record.** Contradiction: directly and explicitly excluded — "Outcome does not assert... success, failure." **Disposition: Rejected outright** (Section 21).

**Candidate G — Generic Occurrence Record.** Tested against the architecture-wide process/occurrence exclusion (OE-003 §3: a Domain Event "does not record occurrence in the sense of an external, real-world happening"). Outcome is not a Domain Event; it is a Domain Object whose own canonical Definition supplies an explicit, textually stated exception — recording that "a state of affairs is treated as realized" is precisely the *result*-level fact this architecture permits, distinct from the *process*-level facts it excludes (Section 11). **Disposition: Rejected as a generic occurrence record, but the underlying result-level content is adopted** (Candidate D) precisely because OE-002 supplies its own specific exception rather than because generic occurrence recording is licensed.

**Candidate H — Primitive Settled Result.** Tested: is Outcome best understood as a bare, stipulated type tag (Doctrine §14) the way Reasoning Trace's "support" relation is, with no further definitional content? No — OE-002 §5.6 supplies substantially richer definitional content than a bare primitive (the realized/actual-versus-intended/possible/proposed distinction, the explicit causal/success/failure/measurement exclusions, the explicit retrospective-recording permission) — this is definitional content in its own right, not merely a label. **Disposition: Rejected as the sole characterization**, though "realization" is still properly treated as an architecturally stipulated term of art (Doctrine §14), not imported wholesale from ordinary-language usage about business results (Section 12).

**Candidate I — Judgment About Result.** Tested for semantic collapse: Outcome and Judgment have textually distinct definitions (realized state of affairs vs. characterization of a subject), distinct grammar (Section 32), and are each independently Final-adopted, closed-set members. **Disposition: Rejected** — no collapse is warranted or supported (Section 16).

**Candidate J — Identity Shell.** Tested: does an Outcome containing only `id`, `case_id`, `recorded_at` (no realized-matter content whatsoever) leave a complete Outcome? No — OE-002's own Definition requires "a determinate state of affairs" to be identifiable as realized, which an empty shell cannot supply. **Disposition: Rejected** as insufficient alone (Section 33, Test 2).

**Candidate K — Adopted but Structurally Deferred.** Tested and found not to apply: OE-002 §5.6 supplies unusually rich, directly derivable content (Sections 9–24); the only genuinely open matter (internal-versus-referential coexistence) is narrow, already-precedented, and does not block a faithful minimal contract, exactly as it did not block Judgment or Decision. **Disposition: Rejected** as the final outcome.

## 8. Contradiction Analysis

No contradiction exists within the governing track regarding Outcome. The one internally-acknowledged open question (both-forms-coexisting) is explicitly disclosed by OE-002 itself, not a contradiction, and is structurally identical in kind to Judgment's and Decision's own analogous deferrals — already correctly handled, in this document, by direct inheritance of the corrected Decision contract (Section 32) rather than by re-introducing the same XOR error a third time.

## 9. Selected Ontological Meaning

**An Outcome is a permanent, independently-identified, Case-owned Domain Object that records a determinate state of affairs which the Case treats as having become actual — its realized matter being either content held internally within the Outcome or a reference to another same-Case, already-accepted Domain Object of any adopted type — without asserting objective truth, without attributing that realization to a specific cause, and without asserting success, failure, or measurement.** This is Candidate D (alternative content forms), informed by Candidate H's partial primitivist framing of "realization" as a term of art, rejecting Candidates B (as mandatory, causal, or exclusive to Decision), C (as textually unsupported), E, F, G (as a generic occurrence record), I, and J.

## 10. Domain Object Status

Final-adopted (Section 4); member of OE-002 §4's closed set; independently identified (OE-002 §5.6 Identity clause); permanent (INV-009); belongs to exactly one Case (INV-002); referenceable by other Domain Objects (explicit — "Outcome MAY be referenced by a Decision or by a Reasoning Trace as one permissible target among others"); subject to acceptance under the general OE-005/OE-006 model (confirmed to contain no Outcome-specific rule, Section 3); governed directly by INV-002 through INV-006 and INV-012, and conditionally by INV-004/INV-005 when the referential form is used. Nothing in Outcome's status is inferred merely because it appears last in OE-002 §5's ordering (Section 6).

## 11. Result Versus Occurrence

Outcome records a **result** — a determinate state of affairs the Case treats as realized — not the bare fact that some process or transition occurred. This distinction matters because Domain Events are architecturally restricted: OE-003 §3 states a Domain Event "does not record occurrence in the sense of an external, real-world happening, and it does not record a process or transition beyond the single fact that acceptance took place." `OutcomeAccepted` fully respects this restriction — it records only that Outcome's *acceptance* occurred, exactly like every other Domain Event, per OE-003 §4.6's own "Fact established" clause. The Outcome *object itself*, however, is not a Domain Event; it is a Domain Object whose own Definition clause supplies a specific, textually stated exception permitting it to carry result-level content — "a determinate state of affairs which the Case treats as having become actual," explicitly distinguished from "intended, possible, or proposed matter." This is not a generic license to record occurrences or processes; it is a narrow, canonically supplied exception for exactly one kind of content (a realized state of affairs), and this document does not generalize it beyond what OE-002 §5.6 itself states. Outcome does not violate the process-exclusion boundary because it never purports to record a process, chronology, or stepwise transition — only a settled, realized state of affairs, precisely the same category of content Judgment records for characterization and Decision records for commitment.

## 12. Outcome Content

Outcome's realized matter, when internal, is free-form text (a `Statement`-like, non-empty-string value object, matching the already-established codebase convention and directly reusing the existing `Statement` value object at `atlas/core/domain/outcome/value_objects.py`), sufficient on its own to constitute the complete realized matter — genuinely semantic, since it *is* the state of affairs the Case treats as actual, per OE-002's own Definition clause. No structured measurement, success/failure classification, or evaluative vocabulary is canonically required or licensed (Sections 21–22); OE-002 imposes no structure beyond requiring that *some* determinate state of affairs be identifiable as realized. Arbitrary or generic JSON is rejected for the same reason it was rejected for Decision: it would postpone rather than satisfy the forced minimum-content requirement, smuggling in unbounded, unvalidated structure with no canonical basis. "Realization" itself is treated as an architecturally stipulated term (Doctrine §14, informed by Candidate H) — not imported from business, financial, or product usage about "outcomes," "results," or "returns."

## 13. Subject or Target Semantics

Outcome's realized matter need not be about anything external at all — the internal-content form requires no separate target. Where the referential form is used, the reference: MUST be to another same-Case, already-accepted Domain Object (INV-004/INV-005); is typed (`matter_target_type`/`matter_target_id`); has no restriction to any specific Domain Object type ("No specific Domain Object type is required" — OE-002 names none as illustrative examples for Outcome specifically, unlike Decision, which names Judgment/Reasoning Trace/Outcome; this absence of named examples does not narrow the rule, since the operative text — "No specific Domain Object type is required" — is unconditional); is zero-or-one in cardinality (there is at most one realized-matter reference per Outcome); contributes to no dedicated dedup rule; and is structurally incapable of self-reference or cycles, for the identical DAG argument already established for every prior type in this series (an object cannot reference itself before it exists, and INV-005 forbids referencing anything not already accepted, which forecloses cycles by construction). Target identity does not contribute to Outcome's own identity (OE-002 §5.6 Identity clause: "independent... of the identity of any object it references").

## 14. Relationship to Decision

**No mandatory relationship in either direction; two independent, generic possibilities, both explicitly disclaimed as required, and neither implying causation, order, or a back-reference.** OE-002 §5.6 states directly: "Outcome does not require a Decision." Symmetrically, OE-002 §5.5 states "Decision does not require a prior Judgment, Reasoning Trace, or Outcome." Where a relationship exists at all, it runs in exactly one of two independent, generic directions:

- **Decision referencing Outcome** — a Decision may, in its own referential committed-to-matter form, use an already-accepted Outcome as its target (one of three named illustrative examples in OE-002 §5.5, "no specific Domain Object type is required" beyond that). This means the Case is committing itself regarding that already-realized Outcome — nothing more. It does **not** mean the Decision caused the Outcome; OE-002 explicitly forecloses causal attribution for Outcome regardless of what references it (Section 24). It does **not** establish any ordering requirement between the two beyond INV-005's prior-acceptance rule (the Outcome must already be accepted before the Decision can reference it — but this says nothing about which state of affairs, in real-world time, happened "first"; a Decision may reference a retrospectively-recorded Outcome whose realized matter is timed well before the Decision's own `decided_at`, if any). It does **not** establish any back-reference from Outcome to that Decision.
- **Outcome referencing Decision** — symmetrically, Outcome's own realized-matter reference MAY, generically, target an already-accepted Decision, since "No specific Domain Object type is required" for Outcome's reference either. This is Outcome treating some already-settled commitment as itself the realized matter (e.g., a Decision whose commitment is now treated, by the Case, as having become actual in some further sense) — an ordinary instance of the generic reference rule, not a distinguished "Outcome-of-a-Decision" relationship.

One Decision may be the generic target of any number of separate Outcomes (no uniqueness constraint is canonically supported, mirroring every other reference relationship in this series); one Outcome may, at most, generically target one other Domain Object as its own realized matter (Outcome's reference cardinality is zero-or-one, Section 13) — it cannot "concern multiple Decisions" through its own reference slot, though nothing prevents multiple, independent Decisions from each separately referencing the same Outcome. An Outcome can exist entirely without any Decision anywhere in the Case (the internal-content form requires none at all).

## 15. Decision Referencing Outcome

Reusing and precisely bounding the Decision Implementation Design's own finding: a Decision referencing an Outcome commits the Case regarding that Outcome specifically. This is a generic instance of Decision's own unrestricted reference rule, not a distinguished "Decision-executes-Outcome" mechanism. It does not automatically mean the Decision caused the Outcome (Outcome's own Definition forecloses causal attribution regardless of which object references it, or how). It does not automatically mean the Outcome occurred before or after the Decision in real-world time — only that the Outcome must already be *accepted* (INV-005) before the Decision's event may occur, which is an acceptance-ordering fact, not a represented-time fact (OE-003 §9 draws this distinction explicitly, and it applies here without modification). It does not establish that Outcome has a back-reference to the Decision that referenced it — Outcome's own definition names no such reciprocal field, and none is added here without canonical basis (Section 34).

## 16. Relationship to Judgment

**No mandatory edge.** OE-002 §5.6 does not name Judgment as a required precondition ("Outcome does not require... a Judgment"), and OE-002 §5.4 does not name Outcome as a required precondition for Judgment either. Where a relationship exists, it is exactly the same generic, unrestricted reference mechanism already described for Decision (Section 14): a Judgment may reference an Outcome as its own subject (one of Judgment's own permitted forms), and Outcome may reference a Judgment as its own realized matter (since "No specific Domain Object type is required" for Outcome's reference). Neither is required; neither creates a distinguished "Judgment-evaluates-Outcome" or "Outcome-confirms-Judgment" relationship — no canonical text states that a Judgment predicts, evaluates, confirms, or falsifies an Outcome, and none is invented here.

**Distinguishing Outcome's content from Judgment's `characterization`, if Outcome carries free-text content:** the two are distinguished by the differing *nature of the claim*, not by any structural or type-level marker beyond the object's own `id`/event type. Judgment's `characterization` is an **assessment, status, or position** the Case holds *about* a subject — an epistemic, evaluative speech act (OE-002 §5.4: "records the Case's settled, Case-relative characterization"). Outcome's realized content is a **description of a state of affairs treated as actual** — a constative, realization-reporting speech act, explicitly stripped of evaluative content by its own Responsibility clause ("does not assert... success, failure"). Put precisely: Judgment says "the Case holds this characterization to be the case"; Outcome says "the Case treats this state of affairs as having become actual," with no assessment of whether that state of affairs is good, bad, successful, or expected. Two free-text fields with identical surface content but different governing types therefore differ exactly in which of these two speech acts they perform — a distinction anchored directly in each type's own Definition and Responsibility clauses, not invented for this document (Section 33, Test 4, reuses the identical grammar-comparison and type-tag discipline already established for Decision versus Judgment).

## 17. Relationship to Observation

Observation **is** an independently adopted Domain Object (OE-002 §5.1), not a synonym for Outcome, not internal Outcome content, and not merely a source for Outcome. Observation "preserves informational content without asserting that the content is true"; Outcome "records a determinate state of affairs which the Case treats as having become actual" without asserting truth, causation, success, failure, or measurement. These are textually distinct responsibilities: Observation's disclaimer concerns the *truth of preserved content in general*; Outcome's disclaimers concern *truth, causation, success, failure, and measurement specifically of a realized state of affairs*. Neither definition states that Outcome derives from, requires, or is a specialization of Observation. Outcome MAY generically reference an Observation as its realized-matter target (an ordinary instance of the unrestricted reference rule, Section 13), and Observation MAY generically be referenced by any Domain Object including Outcome — but no special "Outcome observes X" relationship is adopted. Treating Outcome as inherently "observational" in meaning — implying perception, witnessing, or measurement-derived content — would add unsupported epistemic semantics beyond what OE-002 §5.6 states; this document does not do so.

## 18. Relationship to Reasoning Trace

**No mandatory relation in either direction.** Two independent, generic possibilities exist, neither required: (1) Outcome MAY generically be one of a Reasoning Trace's supporting Domain Objects, since Reasoning Trace places no type restriction on its supporters (OE-002 §5.3: "Reasoning Trace MAY be supported by one or more other Domain Objects defined in this document"); (2) Outcome's own realized-matter reference MAY, generically, target a Reasoning Trace (one of Decision's three named examples applies equally to Outcome's own unrestricted reference rule, since Outcome's text states "No specific Domain Object type is required"). Neither possibility requires Outcome to have "reasoning support," and neither turns Reasoning Trace into an explanation, provenance chain, causal history, or validation mechanism for Outcome — the finalized Reasoning Trace design (primitive support relation directed at the Trace itself) is not altered or reinterpreted by this document.

## 19. Relationship to Knowledge Reference

Symmetric and generic, exactly as established for Decision. Knowledge Reference "MAY reference any other Domain Object... No specific Domain Object type is required as its target," so a Knowledge Reference MAY target an Outcome, exactly as it may target any of the six adopted types. Symmetrically, Outcome's own realized-matter reference could, in principle, target a Knowledge Reference, since Outcome's own "No specific Domain Object type is required" disclaimer excludes no type. Neither relationship is required; neither is a special, dedicated edge distinct from each type's own already-established generic reference rule.

## 20. Relationship to Learning

**Learning is not an adopted Domain Object and is not a governing concept.** `atlas/core/domain/learning/entity.py` implements a legacy, pre-OE-002 CoreLoop concept that is not a member of OE-002 §4's closed six-type set (Observation, Knowledge Reference, Reasoning Trace, Judgment, Decision, Outcome — no seventh type exists). This is structurally identical to the already-confirmed non-adoption of Reasoning Act: the governing architecture deliberately excludes process, occurrence, and retrospective-workflow records beyond what OE-002 §5.6 itself specifically licenses for Outcome (Section 11). No relationship between Outcome and "Learning" is adopted, tested, or invented here — this document does not infer a retrospective learning workflow ("Learning requires Outcome," "Outcome produces Learning," or any comparison structure) because no canonical material defines one. If a future, dedicated architectural investigation adopts Learning as a genuine seventh Domain Object type, that adoption — and Learning's relationship to Outcome, if any — would need its own OE-002 amendment under the Architecture Doctrine's own change protocol (Section 2's "Definition of the Domain Object Model" states the set is closed); it is out of scope here and not fabricated.

## 21. Evaluation, Success, and Failure

**No evaluative content of any kind is adopted, and none is added.** This is the single most directly and explicitly textually excluded matter in this entire investigation: OE-002 §5.6's own Responsibility clause states, without qualification, "Outcome does not assert causality, success, failure, or measurement." No `success`, `failure`, `partial_success`, `favorable`/`unfavorable`, `achieved`/`not_achieved`, `performance_score`, `variance`, or `quality` field is included. What happened (the realized matter itself) is kept strictly distinct from how it is characterized or whether it was desirable — the latter belongs, if it is recorded at all, to a separate Judgment (which MAY reference the Outcome as its own subject, Section 16), never to Outcome itself. Atlas does not, and structurally cannot, endorse any evaluation of an Outcome through the Outcome object itself.

## 22. Measurement and Numeric Values

**No measurement model is adopted, and Outcome is not necessarily, or even presumptively, numeric.** The same Responsibility clause directly excludes measurement by name. No `value`, `unit`, `currency`, `percentage`, `duration`, `quantity`, or structured-metrics field is included. A purely qualitative Outcome (free text with no numeric component at all) is fully valid and, absent any adopted measurement model, is in fact the *only* form of internal content OE-002 licenses — units are not required because no measurement concept exists to require them; multiple values are not addressed because no single value is addressed; and since no measurement field exists, no question of "changing a value" can bear on Outcome's identity (Section 25) at all. This document does not design a generic metrics system, and any future numeric-measurement need (e.g., for the legacy `Evaluation` concept, Section 20) is out of scope and would require its own forcing function under Doctrine §8.

## 23. Expected Versus Realized

**Outcome represents only realized matter — never expected, intended, forecast, or possible matter.** OE-002 §5.6's own Responsibility clause draws this distinction explicitly: "as distinct from intended, possible, or proposed matter." A desired future condition that a Case commits to bringing about belongs entirely to Decision's own committed-to matter (OE-002 §5.5: "regarding what is to be done") — it is not, and never becomes, an Outcome merely by virtue of being desired, expected, or forecast. Only when the Case comes to treat some state of affairs as **actual** does Outcome's own Definition apply. This boundary is not inferred from the ordinary-language noun "outcome" (which might suggest any eventual result, expected or not); it is stated directly by OE-002's own text.

## 24. Causation

**No causal attribution is adopted, is required, or may be added.** OE-002 §5.6's Definition clause states this twice over, independently: "without... attributing that realization to a specific cause" (Definition) and "Outcome does not assert causality" (Responsibility) — reinforced by the general OE-002 §3 rule that no Domain Object "asserts causal attribution... except where explicitly stated otherwise below" (no exception is stated for Outcome). No `caused_by`, `decision_id`-as-causal-link, or any other causal-attribution field is included. An Outcome may exist, fully validly, when its cause is entirely unknown (Test 8, Section 33) — causal silence is not incompleteness, since causation is never part of Outcome's content to begin with. Multiple Decisions or external, unrepresented factors may, as a real-world matter, have contributed to the same realized state of affairs; Outcome represents this without any difficulty and without inventing a causation structure, precisely because it makes no causal claim to reconcile with any number of real-world contributing factors (Test 9, Section 33). Causation, as a subject, is entirely outside Atlas Core's Domain Object Model as it concerns Outcome.

## 25. Identity

`OutcomeId`, independently assigned, stable, and — per OE-002 §5.6's own Identity clause — independent both of the Outcome's own content and of the identity of any object it references. Two Outcomes may contain identical realized content, or reference the identical Domain Object, and remain two distinct, independently accepted Domain Objects — the same conclusion already reached, on parallel textual grounds, for every prior type in this series. Repeated recording of "the same real-world result" (e.g., an interim reading followed by a final one, as the existing implementation's own docstring anticipates) creates a **new**, separate Outcome each time; the earlier one is never mutated, replaced, or deleted (INV-009/011; and explicitly, OE-002 §5.6's own closing sentence: "A later Outcome does not erase or invalidate an earlier one"). No deduplication rule is adopted or invented; semantic duplicates are permitted without qualification.

## 26. Case Ownership

Exactly one Case per Outcome (INV-002), assigned independently before validation and acceptance, never derived from any referenced object, immutable once accepted — the identical, already-established discipline reused here without modification. Where the referential form is used, the referenced object MUST belong to the same Case (INV-004, explicit in OE-002 §5.6's own text: "MUST be to another same-Case Domain Object"). No cross-Case Outcome relation is permitted under any circumstance.

## 27. Actor or Recorder

**No actor, observer, recorder, source, reporter, user, or system field is adopted, and none is added.** No exception to OE-002 §3's blanket "no Domain Object... requires an Agent" rule is stated anywhere in §5.6 (unlike Decision and Judgment, Outcome's own text does not even need to restate this disclaimer, since the general §3 rule already covers it and no exception is carved out). This is independently reinforced by Outcome's own causal-attribution exclusion (Section 24): if Outcome asserted no cause, it would be incoherent for it to nonetheless assert a specific human or system *actor* responsible for the realization. Who caused the result, who observed it, who recorded it, who accepted it, and who owns it (the Case, per Section 26) are kept strictly distinct, and only the last is architecturally relevant to Outcome's own content. No `agent_id`, `recorded_by`, or `source` field is included.

## 28. Truth-Aptness

**Outcome does not assert objective truth, and this is Outcome's own, independently stated disclaimer — not inherited by analogy from Judgment.** OE-002 §5.6's Definition clause states directly: "without asserting objective truth"; OE-003 §4.6 independently confirms: "Not established: the objective truth of the realized matter." An accepted Outcome later shown not to have occurred as recorded (Test 6, Section 33) remains fully ontologically valid — acceptance asserts only that the Case *treats* the realized matter as actual, not that this treatment is objectively, externally verified or correct. Incorrect recording does not invalidate the object's identity (identity is independent of content, Section 25); it does not retroactively un-accept the Outcome (permanence, INV-009/011); it may, at most, motivate a *separate*, later, independently-accepted Outcome or Judgment addressing the correction (Section 30) — never a mutation of the original. Contradictory Outcomes may coexist in the same Case, by the identical no-consistency-requirement reasoning already established for Judgment and Decision.

## 29. Temporal Semantics

**Two distinct times are canonically discussed for Outcome, more explicitly and more repeatedly than for any other type in this series: `recorded_at` (acceptance/event time, forced by INV-015 and the general contract) and the time at which the realized matter is treated as having become actual (represented time), which OE-002 §5.6 itself names directly and which OE-003 discusses across three separate sections (§4.6, §7, §9), and which OE-004's INV-015 references by name.** This is markedly stronger canonical support than Decision's own `decided_at` (which this series found "well-corroborated... but not canonically forced by OE-002's own abstract text alone"): here, OE-002's own Definition-adjacent text and two further normative documents repeatedly discuss this exact temporal distinction as a real, load-bearing feature of Outcome's content, not merely as a plausible timestamp by analogy to Observation or Decision.

This document nonetheless does **not** treat a structured `occurred_at`-equivalent field as strictly *forced* to be present on every instance (non-nullable): OE-002 states only that this represented time, where it applies, "need not equal" acceptance time — it does not state that every Outcome instance must supply a distinct, structured timestamp for it. A purely qualitative realized-matter statement with no separately extracted timestamp remains a fully complete Outcome under OE-002's own Definition, which requires only that a determinate state of affairs be identifiable as realized, not that its exact represented time be separately, structurally recorded. The correct, precisely calibrated conclusion is: an `occurred_at`-equivalent field is **well-corroborated and directly, repeatedly canonically anticipated as a genuine content dimension distinct from `recorded_at`** — adopted here as a **permitted, optional (nullable)** field, present whenever an instance's realized matter carries a determinate represented time, absent otherwise. Where present, INV-015 and OE-003 §9 both require that it never be conflated with, replaced by, or treated as equivalent to `recorded_at` — retrospective recording (an `occurred_at` earlier than `recorded_at`) is explicitly, canonically valid and unremarkable (OE-003 §7). No `effective_at` or `expires_at` field is adopted — nothing in OE-002 supports a distinct "when this realization takes effect" or "when it lapses" concept for Outcome, and both would import unadopted finality/scope semantics absent from §5.6.

## 30. Lifecycle and Mutability

Immutable from acceptance (INV-009/011): no proposed/pending/active/superseded/expired/withdrawn state exists for an accepted Outcome — it either is accepted (permanent) or does not yet exist as a Domain Object. No `status` field is added (Section 21, Section 23). A changed, corrected, or additional realized-matter statement is represented by accepting a new, separate Outcome, never an update to the existing one — directly and explicitly confirmed by OE-002 §5.6's own closing sentence: "A later Outcome does not erase or invalidate an earlier one."

## 31. Corrections and Later Information

When a recorded Outcome is later found incomplete, inaccurate, contradicted, or superseded by better information, the adopted model is: **create a new, separate, independently-accepted Outcome (or, where a characterization/assessment of the original Outcome is what is actually being recorded, a new Judgment referencing it) — never mutate the existing one.** This is not an invented rule; it follows directly from OE-002 §5.6's own explicit closing sentence plus the general permanence invariants (INV-009/010/011), and mirrors the identical "new fact, not a mutation" discipline already established for Knowledge Reference, Reasoning Trace, Judgment, and Decision. No supersession relation, status flag, or deletion mechanism is adopted; where a later Outcome practically supersedes an earlier one, that relationship is purely extrinsic and informal (an ordinary fact about the later Outcome's mere existence), exactly as established for Decision's own supersession treatment (Decision Implementation Design, Section 23).

## 32. Internal Content Versus Reference Form

**This section directly inherits, and independently re-verifies, the corrected Decision Implementation Design's own Committed-To Matter Completeness Review**, since OE-002 §5.6 states, in its own words, that Outcome's realized matter follows "the same pattern as Judgment and Decision." Re-verification confirms the parallel is exact: OE-002 §5.6's Ownership Boundary clause uses the identical sentence structure as §5.5's own clause ("MAY be content held internally... or a reference to another Domain Object belonging to the same Case. Both forms are independently valid. This document does not establish that both forms may coexist... this is an open question under Section 7 of the Architecture Doctrine"), differing only in substituting "realized matter" for "committed-to matter" and "Outcome" for "Decision." Applying the identical Required Distinctions already tested for Decision (and not re-derived a third time from first principles):

- **Alternative meaning versus exclusive representation**: the "or" here, as for Decision, does not license an XOR; it combines minimum-sufficiency ("either form alone suffices") with genuine, stated underdetermination about combination.
- **Ontology versus schema capacity**: the database MAY store both realized-matter forms populated; the domain constructor MAY accept a structurally well-formed candidate presenting both; the acceptance boundary today does **not** admit a candidate presenting both as a valid Outcome; the public API mirrors the acceptance boundary.
- **Minimum Valid Outcome**: internal content only — explicitly valid; typed reference only — explicitly valid; both — unresolved at the ontology level, representable but not currently admissible; neither — explicitly invalid, forced by OE-002's own Definition requiring a determinate, identifiable realized state of affairs.
- **Combined form semantics**: no canonical text adopts any interpretation for content-and-reference presented together (whether they must agree, whether one qualifies the other, whether one is documentary) — the same absence of an adopted interpretation found for Decision, for the same reason: neither §5.5 nor §5.6 states one.
- **Persistence Constraint — Candidate Evaluation**: Candidate A (Strict XOR) is rejected for the identical reason it was rejected for Decision — it would silently decide, in the negative, an open question OE-002 explicitly preserves. Candidate B (Inclusive OR with assigned meaning) is rejected — it would invent an unadopted combined-form interpretation. **Candidate C (Storage-Permissive, Domain-Restrictive) is adopted** — the schema stores either or both forms without a forbidding constraint; domain validation and acceptance admit only the two independently-established forms today; the combined form is reserved, representable, and currently unadmitted. Candidate D (Separate Representation Variants) is rejected as unnecessary, for the identical reason given for Decision. Candidate E (Structurally Deferred) is rejected as too strong, for the identical reason given for Decision.
- **API Contract**: create/capture accepts content-only or reference-only; rejects both together with a distinctly-labeled, non-numbered "combined form not currently admitted" finding — never a generic malformed-input error and never a silent acceptance.

**Corrected minimal contract, stated directly (not merely by cross-reference)**: Outcome's realized matter must be read as **at least one of** {internal content, a typed reference} — never a required content field *plus* an independently optional reference. Whether the two forms may coexist within a single instance is explicitly, deliberately left open by OE-002 itself, exactly as for Decision, and this document does not resolve it — the minimum contract (at least one form present) is fully statable without resolving it, and the combined form is storage-representable but domain-unadmitted (Section 38, Section 39).

## 33. Acceptance

Acceptance of an Outcome means exactly what the general OE-006 contract states: the object is admitted into permanent, accepted Case state, and its corresponding `OutcomeAccepted` event has occurred, atomically. **Acceptance does not assert**: that the described state of affairs objectively occurred (Section 28); any cause of the realization (Section 24); success, failure, or any measurement (Sections 21–22); that a target was achieved in any evaluative sense; or that Atlas endorses any characterization of the realization. Acceptance asserts only that the specified realized matter, in the specified Case, is now a permanent, settled fact of record — that the Case treats it as actual. The realized matter must be fully complete and determinate at acceptance time: at least one of the two forms must be present and well-formed (forced); a candidate presenting **both** forms is not accepted today, not because it violates a forced invariant, but because no interpretation of simultaneous presence has been adopted (Section 32) — such a candidate is classified Invalid, with a distinctly-labeled, non-numbered finding, never silently accepted and never treated as an ordinary INV-nnn violation. Where the referential form is used, the referenced object must already be accepted (INV-004/005; OE-003 §4.6's own stated precondition).

## 37. Reference Semantics

Reused directly from the established Knowledge Reference/Reasoning Trace/Judgment/Decision pattern: **typed** (`matter_target_type`/`matter_target_id`); **no type restriction** — OE-002 §5.6 names no illustrative examples at all for Outcome's own reference (unlike Decision, which names three), and states "No specific Domain Object type is required" unconditionally, so this design does not narrow the reference to any subset of the six adopted types; **same-Case required** (INV-004); **prior-acceptance required** (INV-005); **duplicate references** — not applicable, since there is at most one realized-matter reference per Outcome; **role** — a single, fixed role ("realized matter"); **order** — not applicable, singular; **self-reference** — structurally impossible; **cycles** — structurally impossible (identical DAG argument, established repeatedly in this series); **referential integrity** — enforced at the domain-validation layer, not via SQL foreign key (Section 38); **deletion behavior** — not applicable, permanence forbids it.

## 38. Persistence Design

One table, `outcomes_v2` (or an eventual migration of the existing `outcomes` table, per Section 42): `id` (PK, `String`), `case_id` (indexed `String`), `realized_content` (nullable `String`), `matter_target_type` (nullable, indexed `String`, CHECK-constrained to the six adopted type names when present), `matter_target_id` (nullable, indexed `String`), `occurred_at` (nullable `String`, ISO-8601, timezone-aware when present), `recorded_at` (`String`).

**Persistence constraint**: a single CHECK constraint enforcing only **minimum presence** — `realized_content IS NOT NULL OR (matter_target_type IS NOT NULL AND matter_target_id IS NOT NULL)` — ruling out only the genuinely forbidden empty case (neither form present). **No constraint forbids both being non-null simultaneously**, for the identical reason established for Decision: a hard XOR would make the combined form structurally impossible to store, silently deciding OE-002's open question in the negative rather than preserving it. Leaving the schema permissive means that if a future OE amendment resolves the question in either direction, no schema migration is required — only the domain-layer validation rule (Section 39) would need to change. No foreign key across the polymorphic reference, for the same reasons established throughout this series (including, specifically, no SQL foreign key from `matter_target_id` to any Decision table, mirroring the existing Outcome table's own already-established no-FK convention for `decision_id`). No deletion, no update method, append-only. camelCase API serialization follows the established shared convention (`atlas.core.infrastructure.api.serialization.CamelModel`, per ADR-004, already used by the Decision and Observation API layers).

## 39. API Consequences

**Create/capture**: accepts (or resolves) a `case_id`, at least one realization of the realized matter, and an optional `occurred_at`. **Validation rule**: (1) reject as `Incomplete` if neither realization is present at all; (2) reject as `Invalid`, carrying a distinctly-labeled, non-numbered "combined form not currently admitted" finding, if *both* realizations are present; (3) if exactly one realization is present, validate it via the same generic algorithm already established for Knowledge Reference, Reasoning Trace, Judgment, and Decision (INV-004/INV-005 when the referential form is used); (4) if `occurred_at` is present, it MUST be a timezone-aware datetime (reusing the existing implementation's own validation), but its absence is not a validation failure. **Read**: required — get by `case_id` + `id`. **List**: permitted, generic (reusing the existing `list_all`/`list_by_decision_id`-style pattern, generalized to `list_by_target` where a reference is used, since the target is no longer Decision-specific). **Update, correct, supersede, revoke**: none is meaningful or adopted — permanence forbids all of them as domain operations; a correction is simply a new capture (Section 31). **Attach a reference after the fact**: not permitted — the realized matter is fixed entirely at capture time, per OE-006 §4's "no candidate element may be replaced" rule. **Delete**: forbidden. This design does not currently exist as a REST API (Section 42); these are the specifications the future API layer must satisfy, not a description of code already present.

## 40. Invariants

**Adopted**: INV-002 (single Case ownership); INV-003/INV-006 (distinct identity); INV-004 (same-Case reference, when the referential form is used); INV-005 (prior acceptance of the referenced matter, when present); INV-007 (exactly one `OutcomeAccepted` event, structurally trivial); INV-008 (atomic acceptance); INV-009/010/011 (permanence, non-erasure — including the explicit non-invalidation-by-later-Outcome rule); INV-012 (conditional root eligibility, depending on chosen form); INV-015 (acceptance time as event time, with the explicit, Outcome-specific carve-out permitting represented time to diverge, Section 29).

**Logically derived**: an Outcome lacking any realized matter (neither form present) is incomplete/Invalid, by direct analogy to Knowledge Reference's own zero-target resolution and to Judgment's and Decision's own analogous completeness rules; self-reference and cycles are structurally impossible; no separate Domain Event artifact is required (OE-003 §3, INV-007); the two realized-matter forms are *not* logically derived to be mutually exclusive — only their *minimum presence* (at least one) is forced; their potential simultaneous coexistence remains a genuinely open, unresolved question (per OE-002 §5.6's own explicit text), treated as currently unadmitted, not invariantly forbidden.

**Proposed implementation constraints**: a minimum-presence CHECK constraint across the content/reference field groups (not an XOR); a domain-layer validation rule rejecting the combined form as Invalid-with-a-distinct-finding, separate from and not itself one of the fifteen numbered invariants; a domain-layer validation rule requiring `occurred_at`, when present, to be timezone-aware and never conflated with `recorded_at`; no foreign key across the polymorphic reference; no `update`/`delete` repository method; no "supersede" or "correct" operation.

**Unresolved candidates**: whether both realized-matter forms may ever coexist within one Outcome instance, and be admitted as such — explicitly, permanently open per OE-002 §5.6 itself, not resolved here, and not blocking, precisely because the adopted design (Storage-Permissive, Admission-Restricted) requires no schema change to accommodate whichever way it is eventually resolved.

## 41. Edge-Case Analysis

**An Outcome with no Decision** — fully valid; Outcome requires no Decision at all (Section 14). **An Outcome relating to multiple Decisions** — not directly representable through Outcome's own single reference slot (zero-or-one), but multiple, independent Decisions may each separately reference the same Outcome without limit; this fully satisfies the practical case without inventing a multi-valued reference on Outcome itself. **A Decision with no later Outcome** — fully valid; nothing in either object's definition requires the other. **An Outcome recorded before a related Decision** — fully valid; acceptance ordering (INV-005) constrains only whether a *referencing* object's target must already be accepted, never which object's *represented* time precedes the other's (Section 14, Section 29). **A Decision referencing an already-existing Outcome** — an ordinary instance of Decision's own generic reference rule (Section 15); requires only that the Outcome already be accepted. **An Outcome with only internal content** — the ordinary internal-content-form case; fully valid, root-eligible. **An Outcome with only a target reference** — the ordinary referential-form case; fully valid, not root-eligible. **An Outcome with both content and reference** — representable at the storage layer (Section 38), but **not currently admitted** by domain validation (Section 33, Section 39): rejected as Invalid with a distinctly-labeled "combined form not currently admitted" finding. **An Outcome with neither** — incomplete/Invalid (Section 33, Section 40); this remains fully, unambiguously forced regardless of the combined-form question. **A qualitative Outcome** — fully valid; no measurement is ever required (Section 22). **A numeric Outcome** — representable only as free text describing a number (e.g., within `realized_content`); no structured numeric field exists, and none is required. **An Outcome with no measurement** — the default, fully valid case; measurement is never adopted content. **An Outcome with no known cause** — fully valid; causation is never part of Outcome's content (Section 24). **An Outcome that was expected but never realized** — never becomes an Outcome at all; it remains, at most, Decision content describing a desired future condition (Section 23). **An accepted Outcome later shown inaccurate** — remains fully, permanently valid as an accepted historical record; acceptance never asserted objective truth (Section 28). **Contradictory Outcomes in one Case** — permitted; no consistency requirement is adopted, by the same reasoning as contradictory Judgments and Decisions. **Duplicate semantic Outcomes under different IDs** — permitted, per Section 25. **Repeated observations of the same result** — each creates a new, separate, permanently coexisting Outcome (Section 25, Section 30). **Cross-Case references** — forbidden (INV-004); yields an Invalid candidate if attempted. **Self-reference** — structurally impossible. **Cyclic Outcome relations** — structurally impossible. **Deletion of a referenced object** — impossible under permanence; nothing is ever deleted. **An anonymous imported Outcome** — fully representable, since no actor field exists to be unknown (Section 27). **Serialization changes without semantic change** — fully compatible; identity and content are independent of presentation.

**Test-by-test resolution**: **Test 1** — removing every Decision relation leaves a complete Outcome; Decision is never required (Section 14). **Test 2** — removing all internal content or result characterization from an Outcome that has no reference either leaves an incomplete object with no determinate realized matter at all; this is not an Outcome, since OE-002's own Definition requires a determinate state of affairs to be identifiable — it is, at most, an unaccepted candidate (Section 40). **Test 3** — internal content alone is canonically complete; no target is required (Section 12, Section 32). **Test 4** — an Outcome and a Judgment given identical free-text fields and no other distinction remain machine-verifiably distinct by their differing accepted type (`OutcomeAccepted` versus `JudgmentAccepted`, INV-001/INV-007) and, at the ontological level, by the differing speech act each performs (realization-report versus characterization, Section 16) — the same discipline already established for Decision versus Judgment. **Test 5** — a desired future condition that never occurs can only ever be Decision content; it cannot be an Outcome, since Outcome requires the Case to treat the matter as already actual (Section 23). **Test 6** — an accepted Outcome later shown not to have occurred as recorded remains fully ontologically valid; acceptance never asserted objective truth (Section 28). **Test 7** — a qualitative result with no numeric value or unit is fully representable as free text; no measurement model is ever required (Section 22). **Test 8** — a settled result with an unknown cause requires nothing further; causation is never part of Outcome's content (Section 24). **Test 9** — multiple contributing Decisions or external events are fully compatible with a single Outcome, precisely because Outcome makes no causal claim to reconcile with them (Section 24). **Test 10** — two Outcomes with identical result content may both exist, distinguished only by identity (Section 25). **Test 11** — without the label `Outcome`, the machine-verifiable distinction from Judgment, Knowledge Reference, a generic note, a measurement, or a Domain Event is the *combination* of a minimum-presence-constrained (not XOR-constrained) realized-matter slot, explicit exclusion of evaluative/causal/measurement content, an optional-but-canonically-anticipated distinct represented-time field, and its own dedicated `OutcomeAccepted` event type — a shape distinct from Judgment (characterization-of-subject, no realization/actuality claim), Knowledge Reference (mandatory single reference, no content, no realization claim), a generic note (no Case ownership, no acceptance model, no invariants), a measurement (no adopted structured-value concept exists here at all), and a bare Domain Event (which records only acceptance, never represented content or represented time, per OE-003 §3). **Test 12** — an accepted Outcome whose recorded result is later shown inaccurate: acceptance asserted exactly that the Case treated the specified realized matter as actual, as of the acceptance time recorded by `recorded_at`; acceptance did **not** assert that this treatment was objectively, externally accurate, verified, or would remain uncontradicted by later information.

## 42. Existing-Code Impact

Outcome already has a substantial existing implementation, inspected in full for this document:

- `atlas/core/domain/outcome/entity.py` implements `Outcome` with fields `id`, `decision_id`, `statement`, `occurred_at`, `recorded_at`, `note`. `atlas/core/domain/outcome/value_objects.py` defines `OutcomeId` and `Statement` (non-empty free text). `atlas/core/infrastructure/persistence/outcome/table.py` defines `outcomes_table` with `decision_id NOT NULL` and `statement NOT NULL`, no SQL foreign key on `decision_id` (already matching this design's own no-FK convention). `atlas/core/application/outcome/capture_outcome.py` mandatorily verifies the referenced Decision exists before construction. **No `atlas/core/infrastructure/api/outcome/` directory exists** — Outcome currently has no REST API layer at all, unlike Decision, Observation, Hypothesis, and Evidence.
- **This is a genuine, three-part existing-code/ontology mismatch, more significant than any found for Decision, and is classified as requiring a future migration, not merely additive compatibility**:
  1. `statement` (content) is **unconditionally required** today. OE-002 §5.6 permits — but never requires — internal content; a reference-only Outcome must also be admissible, and today's schema cannot express one (`statement` is `NOT NULL`).
  2. `decision_id` is **unconditionally required** today. OE-002 §5.6 states directly "Outcome does not require a Decision." Today's schema and constructor make every Outcome mandatorily dependent on an already-accepted Decision, which contradicts the adopted ontology outright, not merely under-specifies it.
  3. Even where a reference exists, it is **hardcoded to Decision only** — no generic `matter_target_type` exists. OE-002 §5.6 states "No specific Domain Object type is required," permitting a reference to any of the six adopted types.
- Taken together, points 1 and 2 mean the existing implementation currently **mandates both forms simultaneously, unconditionally** — the opposite error from Decision's original flawed hard XOR (which *forbade* combination), but an equally unsupported, silently-invented resolution of the same canonically-open combined-form question, since OE-002 never states that content and a Decision reference must always coexist either. This is flagged here as a genuine deviation, not merely an unresolved question left unaddressed.
- **`decided_at`-parallel finding**: `occurred_at` is already present, already required (non-nullable, timezone-validated) in existing code. This is **not** a deviation from the corrected design — Section 29 finds `occurred_at` well-corroborated and permitted; existing code's stricter (mandatory) requirement is a permissible, stricter-than-canonical-minimum implementation policy, not a contradiction, exactly as Decision's own `decided_at` was found to be compatible with, though not forced by, OE-002's abstract text.
- **`note`**: parallels the already-flagged, still-open `decision_type`/`confidence`/`source` question for Decision (Section 35) — not resolved here.
- **Classification**: this design requires a **future migration**, not additive compatibility: `decision_id` must become nullable and non-constitutive (already anticipated by the cited Decision/Outcome Reference Semantics Investigation's own resolution, Section 43); `statement` must become nullable to permit reference-only Outcomes; a generic `matter_target_type`/`matter_target_id` pair must be introduced to replace the Decision-only reference; and a minimum-presence CHECK constraint must replace the current unconditional-AND requirement. No production code was modified in the course of this investigation.

## 43. Unresolved Questions

**Q1 — May both realized-matter forms coexist within a single Outcome instance?** Explicitly and permanently open per OE-002 §5.6 itself; not resolved here, and not resolvable without a genuine forcing function under Doctrine §8. Smallest safe placeholder: enforce only minimum presence (at least one) at the schema level, with no CHECK constraint forbidding both; enforce non-admission of the combined case at the domain-validation layer (Invalid, distinctly labeled, not a numbered-invariant violation), directly inheriting Decision's own corrected placeholder (Section 32, Section 38, Section 39).

**Q2 — Is `note` permissible internal-content structure, or unadopted extra semantics?** No canonical text in OE-002 §5.6 resolves this either way, exactly paralleling the still-open `decision_type`/`confidence`/`source` question for Decision. Smallest safe placeholder: retain as-is, optional, unresolved. Forcing function: a dedicated, future, bounded investigation into this field specifically.

**Q3 — Should a `supersedes_id` reference be added?** Deferred (Section 35); no canonical basis exists today, and none is invented.

**Q4 — Is the "Decision/Outcome Reference Semantics Investigation," cited by the Decision Implementation Design as having already resolved `Outcome.decision_id`'s status, itself present in this repository as a standalone document?** **No.** This document was inspected for and confirmed absent as a separate file (Section 3); its cited conclusion is known only through the Decision Implementation Design's own citations of it. This document relies on that citation only to the extent the Decision Implementation Design itself states it, and does not independently verify or fabricate any content beyond what is already cited there. This is reported as a missing source, not silently assumed away.

None of these questions blocks the minimal design in Section 34 from faithfully preserving OE-002 §5.6's adopted meaning.

## 44. Reopening Conditions

Governed entirely by Architecture Doctrine §8, applied identically to every prior investigation in this series: a genuine forcing function requires a newly identified domain fact inexpressible by the current architecture, an unavoidable contradiction, a downstream normative task exposing a real, demonstrated expressive gap, or evidence the original OE-002 investigation omitted a materially distinct candidate or misapplied the Doctrine's method. Convenience, symmetry with a richer structure, product familiarity with "success," "measurement," or "cause," or a desire to resolve the both-forms-coexisting question preemptively do not qualify.

## 45. Final Implementation Recommendation

Adopt the seven-slot minimal design of Section 34: `id`, `case_id`, a minimum-presence-constrained realized-matter realization (`realized_content` free text, and/or `matter_target_type`/`matter_target_id` typed reference), an optional `occurred_at`, and `recorded_at`. Persist as one table with a single CHECK constraint enforcing only that at least one realization is present — not an XOR — so that the schema can, without future migration, represent a combined-form row whenever the ontology question is eventually resolved. Enforce, at the domain-validation layer only, that ordinary capture and acceptance admit exclusively the content-only and reference-only cases today, rejecting the combined case as Invalid with a distinctly-labeled, non-numbered finding. Reuse the generic reference-validation algorithm already established for Knowledge Reference, Reasoning Trace, Judgment, and Decision where the referential form is used, with no restriction of the reference to Decision specifically. Introduce no Agent, success/failure, measurement, or causal-attribution field — each was tested and rejected on precise, stated canonical grounds, more decisively here than for any other type in this series. Do not resolve the already-flagged `note` question here; it remains a separate, bounded, future task. Reconcile the existing `atlas/core/domain/outcome/` implementation with this minimal design as a genuine future migration effort (Section 42), not as part of this document: `decision_id` and `statement` must both become nullable, and a generic typed-reference pair must replace the Decision-only reference, before the existing code can faithfully admit every form OE-002 §5.6 itself permits.
